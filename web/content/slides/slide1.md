  <h2>Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) with Optical Fiber</h2>
  <h3>A Comprehensive Technical Scientific Review</h3>
  <aside class="notes">
    Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) represents an advanced fiber-optic sensing technology that transforms conventional telecommunication fibers into ultra-dense arrays of acoustic sensors. It enables remote detection of dynamic strain and acoustic vibrations along distances exceeding 100 km with spatial resolutions down to centimeters and sampling rates up to tens of kHz. This presentation provides a detailed scientific and technical overview of DAS, including its physical principles, system design, signal processing, performance characteristics, and applications, supported by recent peer-reviewed literature and standards.
  </aside>

<section>
  <h4>1. Introduction to Distributed Acoustic Sensing</h4>
  <ul style="font-size:large;">
    <li>Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS): Converts standard optical fiber into continuous arrays of acoustic sensors</li>
    <li>Measures dynamic axial strain, strain-rate, and vibrations along entire fiber length</li>
    <li>Spatial resolution: Centimeters to tens of meters; range: >100 km</li>
    <li>Temporal resolution: Real-time with sampling rates up to several kHz</li>
    <li>Applications: Oil & gas, seismology, infrastructure security, environmental monitoring</li>
  </ul>
  <aside class="notes">
    DAS leverages microscopic refractive index inhomogeneities in glass fibers resulting in distributed Rayleigh backscattering centers. The system transmits coherent laser pulses through the fiber and measures phase modulations in the backscattered light induced by external mechanical perturbations. Unlike discrete sensor arrays, DAS provides continuous sensing over kilometer scales with high sensitivity (~pico-strain). Its economic advantage includes utilizing existing telecommunication fiber infrastructure for sensing, enabling widespread deployment. Key references include Terra15 (2025), OFS Optics (2022), and PMC (2022) reviews [1][4][5].
  </aside>
</section>
